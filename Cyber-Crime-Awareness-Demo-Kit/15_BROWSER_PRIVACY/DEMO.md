# 15 · Browser Privacy — "Your browser is talking about you"
**Tech Guardians · AUTHORISED DEMONSTRATION ONLY**

**Objective:** Show how a browser reveals OS, language, timezone, screen, fonts
and more — enough to "fingerprint" and track you without cookies.

**Time Required:** 6–8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Internet:** Required. Offline fallback: screenshots in `../28_MEDIA/SCREENSHOTS/`.

## What You Need
- Browser + the services in `../23_ONLINE_TOOLS/PRIVACY_TOOLS.md`:
  BrowserLeaks, EFF Cover Your Tracks, AmIUnique.

## Step-by-Step
1. Open BrowserLeaks → show IP, OS, browser, screen, timezone, WebGL, fonts.
2. Open AmIUnique → show how "unique" (trackable) your browser is.
3. Open EFF Cover Your Tracks → fingerprinting + tracker-blocking test.
4. Explain: even without login or cookies, this combo can single you out.

## What Audience Sees
A long list of details their browser hands to every website — and how that
uniquely identifies them.

## Presenter Script
"You didn't log in. You didn't accept cookies. Yet this site already knows your
operating system, screen size, timezone, language and more. Combine them and
you're one-in-a-million — which means trackable across sites."

## Security Lesson
Fingerprinting tracks you beyond cookies. Privacy is about reducing how unique
and identifiable you are.

## Prevention
- Use a tracker-blocking browser/extension (uBlock Origin; privacy browsers).
- Keep the browser updated; reduce unnecessary extensions (they leak too).
- Use private windows for sensitive tasks (limits, not perfect).
- Consider the Tor Browser for high-privacy needs (explain trade-offs).
- Be mindful that a VPN hides IP but not browser fingerprint.

## Questions to Ask
- "Did any site ask permission before reading all this?"
- "How unique did AmIUnique say you are?"

## Safety Warning
Demonstrate on **your own** browser/device only.
