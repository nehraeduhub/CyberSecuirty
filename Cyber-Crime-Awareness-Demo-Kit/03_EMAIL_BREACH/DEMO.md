# 03 · Email — "Has your email already leaked?"
**Tech Guardians · AUTHORISED DEMONSTRATION ONLY**

**Objective:** Show how an email address appears in past data breaches, what
data leaked, and why that fuels phishing, password attacks and scams.

**Time Required:** 5–7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Internet:** Required (breach lookups). Offline fallback: `SCREENSHOTS/`.

## What You Need
- Your **own** email, or a fictional sample from `SAMPLE_DATA/`.
- Services: Have I Been Pwned, Mozilla Monitor, EmailRep
  (official URLs in `../23_ONLINE_TOOLS/EMAIL_OSINT.md`).

## Step-by-Step
1. Enter **your own** email (or the sample) into Have I Been Pwned.
2. Read the list of breaches it appears in and **what data types** leaked
   (passwords, phone, DOB, etc.).
3. Cross-check with Mozilla Monitor; show EmailRep's reputation view.
4. Explain the attacker's next move: credential stuffing + targeted phishing.

## What Audience Sees
Their everyday email tied to real past breaches — proof this isn't hypothetical.

## Presenter Script
"Imagine a shop you bought from years ago got breached. Your email and an old
password are now on a list. A criminal tries that password on your bank, your
email, your UPI app. If you reused it — they're in. That's why one leak matters."

## Security Lesson
Breaches are permanent and cumulative. Email is the **master key** (it resets
every other account), so protect it first.

## Prevention
- Unique password per site + a password manager (demo 14).
- Turn on MFA, especially on your email.
- Subscribe to breach alerts (HIBP / Mozilla Monitor).
- Treat "your account was breached" emails as possible phishing — verify directly.

## Questions to Ask
- "How many of you use this email password elsewhere?"
- "If someone had your email login, what else could they reset?"

## Safety Warning
Look up **your own** email or the fictional sample only. Never enter an audience
member's address without their explicit consent.
