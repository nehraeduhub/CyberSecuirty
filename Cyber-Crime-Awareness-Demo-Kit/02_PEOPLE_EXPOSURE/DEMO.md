# 02 · People Exposure — "A name becomes a profile"
**Tech Guardians · AUTHORISED DEMONSTRATION ONLY**

**Objective:** Show how scattered public information about one person can be
combined (correlated) into a profile an attacker uses for social engineering.

**Time Required:** 6–8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Internet:** Required for live lookups · Offline fallback: `../28_MEDIA/`

## What You Need
- The fictional identity in `SAMPLE_IDENTITIES`-style data (use `STORY.md`).
- Browser. (Optional) `../23_ONLINE_TOOLS/PEOPLE_OSINT.md` service list.

## Step-by-Step
1. Introduce the **fictional** person "Aarav Mehta" (see `STORY.md`).
2. Start with one public fact (a social handle).
3. Show how that links to a photo → a city → a workplace → a routine.
4. Assemble the facts on screen into a single "profile card".
5. Ask: *"Now how would a scammer use this to sound convincing?"*

## What Audience Sees
Separate harmless-looking facts snapping together into a profile that feels
"stalker-level" — the aha moment.

## Presenter Script
"None of these facts is secret. A photo here, a check-in there, a public bio.
On their own, nothing. Together? The caller now knows your city, your employer,
your dog's name — enough to make you believe he's legitimate."

## Security Lesson
Attackers don't "hack" first — they **research** first. Correlation of public
data (OSINT) powers the convincing part of most scams.

## Prevention
- Lock down social privacy settings; limit public posts.
- Don't reuse the same username everywhere (see demo 04).
- Remove home/work/location details from public bios.
- Assume anything public can be combined with everything else public.

## Questions to Ask
- "Which of these facts have you posted publicly?"
- "If a caller knew these, would you trust them more?"

## Safety Warning
Use only the **fictional** identity provided, or your **own** data with consent.
Never profile an audience member or a real third party.
