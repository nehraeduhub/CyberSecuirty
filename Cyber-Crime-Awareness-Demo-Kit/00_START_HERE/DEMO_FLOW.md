# Demo Flow — the story-first method

> **Never** open with: "Today I will show you Nmap."
> **Always** open with: "Imagine you receive this message…"

## The 7-beat structure for every demonstration
1. **Hook (story):** describe a situation the audience recognises.
2. **What the victim sees:** show the message / screen / call script.
3. **Why it works:** the emotion (fear, greed, urgency, authority, curiosity).
4. **Reveal the tech:** now show the tool/technique the criminal used.
5. **Red flags:** the 3–5 signals that give it away.
6. **Protect:** the exact steps the audience should take.
7. **One-line takeaway:** something they'll remember tomorrow.

## The emotional levers criminals pull (name them out loud)
- **Authority** — "This is the CBI / your bank / the police."
- **Urgency** — "Act in 10 minutes or your account is blocked."
- **Fear** — "A case has been registered against you."
- **Greed/reward** — "You won / here's your refund."
- **Trust/familiarity** — "Hi, it's me, I lost my phone."

## Pace for a live room
- Keep each demo **5–8 minutes**.
- Pause after the "reveal" — let it land.
- Always finish on **protection**, never on the attack.
- Ask the audience questions (see `../01_SEMINAR_GUIDE/AUDIENCE_INTERACTION.md`).

## Recommended order (builds intensity)
Exposure (02–07) → everyday scams (09,12,10) → the big fear scam (11 Digital
Arrest) → technical awareness (14,16,15) → "what to do if it happens"
(`../25_INCIDENT_RESPONSE/`). End with the 10 safety rules handout.
