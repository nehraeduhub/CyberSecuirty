# 10 · WhatsApp Scam — "Account takeover & contact targeting"
**Tech Guardians · AUTHORISED DEMONSTRATION ONLY**

**Objective:** Show how a WhatsApp account gets taken over (via OTP/QR tricks)
and how the attacker then targets the victim's contacts.

**Time Required:** 6–8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner
**Internet:** Not required (use mock screens). No real account is touched.

## What You Need
- Mock chat screens in `MOCK_MESSAGES/`.
- `INSTRUCTOR_SCRIPT.md`.

## Step-by-Step
1. Show the "fake warning" message (`MOCK_MESSAGES/`).
2. Walk the takeover chain: urgency → "share the code you just got" → account lost.
3. Show the attacker then messaging the victim's family: "I'm stuck, send money."
4. Explain WhatsApp registration-code / linked-device abuse at a high level.

## What Audience Sees
A friend's "hacked" account messaging them for money — and how it started with one
shared 6-digit code.

## Presenter Script
"You get a message: 'Oops, I accidentally sent you my WhatsApp code, please send
it back.' It feels like helping a friend. But that 6-digit code is the key to
YOUR WhatsApp. Share it, and the scammer moves into your account — and then asks
all your contacts for money, as you."

## Security Lesson
The WhatsApp registration code / linked-device QR is a key. Social engineering —
not hacking — is how most accounts are lost.

## Prevention
- **Never share your WhatsApp 6-digit code** with anyone, for any reason.
- Turn on **two-step verification** (PIN) in WhatsApp settings.
- Check **Linked Devices** regularly; remove unknown ones.
- If a contact asks for money/OTP, **call them** to verify.
- Agree a family **safe word** for money requests.

## Questions to Ask
- "Would you recognise a 'send me the code' trick now?"
- "Is your WhatsApp two-step PIN turned on?"

## Safety Warning
Use mock screens only. Do not access, link, or attempt to take over any real
WhatsApp account — including with consent, it's unnecessary for the lesson.
